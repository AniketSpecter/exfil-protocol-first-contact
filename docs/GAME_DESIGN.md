# Game design notes

## Intent

Deliver a compact tactical extraction loop in which the player decides what evidence survives. EXFIL PROTOCOL: FIRST CONTACT is a prequel concept by Aniket [UGZ], following Viper One before the squad loses contact with command.

## Core loop

Brief → deploy → navigate cover and patrols → recover evidence and optional supplies → transmit or carry → extract → unlock the next lead.

Transmitting while stationary protects evidence from loss but reveals a location for enemies to investigate. Carrying it preserves stealth and risks losing the packet on failure. Unique evidence IDs prevent repeat banking from farming XP. Valuable loot rewards successful extraction rather than kills.

## Two playable contracts

Silent Landing introduces the coast, the shipping manifest and safehouse extraction. Success unlocks a shorter evidence uplink and Dead Frequency. The second mission has a separate lighthouse island, boat transport, radio logs and optional surveillance shutdown. Its story suggests the enemy knew the squad's orders in advance.

## Readiness and movement

Primary, secondary and knife slots keep weapon choices readable. Enemy drops offer an ammunition or loadout decision without a large inventory. A knife grants 22% faster normal movement but requires close-range attacks; switching back to a firearm restores ranged readiness. Quiet movement remains slow.

## Recovery and squad scope

Solo has one self-revive, requiring a safe held action before bleed-out. Squad adds two companions that follow, shoot and attempt safe revival. Both modes permit one player recovery per raid. Limited recovery preserves consequences while allowing a first mistake to be recoverable.

## Mobile usability

Two simultaneous thumb controls separate movement from aim/fire. Phone screens use action labels rather than keyboard mappings. A visible medkit button, three-slot selector and editable layout support different preferences. Long screen content scrolls within a bounded area while primary actions remain visible. Portrait gameplay pauses and asks the player to rotate.

## Validation limits

Automated full routes verify the loop and its state transitions, not retention, fun, accessibility or human difficulty. A roughly 3–5 minute first-session goal remains a design target, not a measured result. Physical Android/iOS testing and human play sessions are the next validation steps. There are no purchases, analytics or multiplayer in this build.
